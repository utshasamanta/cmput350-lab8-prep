The rectangle is 400 pixels wide and 300 pixels high. Its corners are:

top-left: (200, 150)
bottom-left: (200, 450)
bottom-right: (600, 450)
top-right: (600, 150)

The two triangles, with their vertices in counterclockwise order, are:

top-left: (200, 150), (200, 450), (600, 150)
bottom-right: (200, 450), (600, 450), (600, 150)

An index buffer can store the four positions once in the order top-left,
bottom-left, bottom-right, top-right. The six indices are:

0, 1, 3, 1, 2, 3

Without an index buffer, the six positions use 6 * 2 * 4 = 48 bytes. With an
index buffer, the four positions use 4 * 2 * 4 = 32 bytes and the six indices
use 6 * 4 = 24 bytes, for a total of 56 bytes. Therefore, the non-indexed version
uses less memory for this quad. In general, indexing is better when vertices are
reused enough that the saved position data is larger than the index data.

With doubles, the non-indexed version uses 6 * 2 * 8 = 96 bytes. The indexed
version uses 4 * 2 * 8 + 6 * 4 = 88 bytes, so the indexed version uses less
memory.

Therefore, the normalized UV coordinates are approximately (0.75, 0.5033).
